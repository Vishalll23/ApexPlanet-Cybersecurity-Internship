
# Common Cybersecurity Attack Vectors

## Introduction
An attack vector is a method or pathway that attackers use to exploit vulnerabilities and gain unauthorized access to systems, networks, or data.

## 1. Social Engineering

Social engineering is a technique in which attackers manipulate people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security.

**Common Techniques:**
- Phishing emails
- Fake phone calls (Vishing)
- SMS phishing (Smishing)
- Impersonation

**Example:**
An attacker pretends to be a bank employee and asks a user to share their OTP or banking credentials.

**Prevention:**
- Never share passwords or OTPs.
- Verify the identity of callers and senders.
- Avoid suspicious links and attachments.
- Provide security awareness training.

## 2. Wireless Attacks

Wireless attacks target Wi-Fi networks and wireless communication to intercept data, gain unauthorized access, or disrupt connectivity.

**Common Types:**
- Evil Twin Attack
- Wi-Fi Eavesdropping
- Rogue Access Point
- Deauthentication Attack

**Example:**
An attacker creates a fake Wi-Fi hotspot with a name similar to a legitimate public network to trick users into connecting.

**Prevention:**
- Use WPA2 or WPA3 encryption.
- Avoid sensitive activities on untrusted public Wi-Fi.
- Use strong Wi-Fi passwords.
- Keep router firmware updated.

## 3. Insider Threats

An insider threat occurs when someone with authorized access to an organization's systems or data misuses that access, intentionally or accidentally.

**Types:**
- Malicious Insider: Deliberately steals or leaks information.
- Negligent Insider: Accidentally exposes sensitive information.
- Compromised Insider: An employee's account is taken over by an attacker.

**Example:**
An employee copies confidential company information to a personal device without authorization.

**Prevention:**
- Apply the Principle of Least Privilege.
- Monitor access logs.
- Use Multi-Factor Authentication.
- Conduct regular security awareness training.

## Comparison of Attack Vectors

| Attack Vector | Target | Main Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Social Engineering | People | Credential theft |
| Wireless Attacks | Wi-Fi networks | Unauthorized access |
| Insider Threats | Organizational systems | Data leakage |

## Conclusion
Understanding attack vectors helps organizations identify possible entry points for cyberattacks and implement suitable security controls.
